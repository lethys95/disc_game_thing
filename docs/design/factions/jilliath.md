Generally speaking, inquisition's overall strategy is to make playing the game painful for the enemy.

The units are very binary in terms of whether or not they're shock or defensive. A lot to do with health manipulation, using it as a resource.

A lot with ultimatums, putting the enemy in a position, where they must make difficult decisions.

Spells: targeting infrastructure a lot, making it easier to conquer territory.

Healing: most healing of any faction, with side effects (martyrdom).

Jilliath is the holy war made flesh: fanatic angels and humans marching under a banner of righteous fire. The Inquisition commits where other factions hesitate. Its identity is aggression, conviction, and the willingness to spend its own ranks to keep momentum.

Central melee dichotomy: **faith preserves versus faith consumes**.

- The preserving line emphasizes protection, healing, and holding ground.
- The consuming line emphasizes self-sacrifice, fanaticism, and martyrdom.

Branch investment makes this a permanent faction-level commitment (see `../pillars.md`).

Martyrdom is faction-wide rather than healer-only. A mage may sacrifice health for an overwhelming attack, while another unit may sacrifice itself to heal an ally. The same doctrine powers both offense and support.
