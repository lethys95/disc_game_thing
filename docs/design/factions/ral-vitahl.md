# Ral-Vitahl — The Nexus

> Name: the user couldn't recall the first half; "Ral-Vitahl, Nexus" is the filename in the user's own 2024 vault (committed 2024-07-29), so it's most likely theirs.

## User's notes
Core identity (user, 2026-09-25): **Expedience and burst.**

Mana color: **Teal** — "Tech. Humans, Automata, Lightning and explosions. Izzet basically." (Unreal attempt, `Mana.as`, 2024). Teal — think lightning (user, 2026-09-25).

Difficulty: hard.

Generally speaking, nexus is about quality over quantity. Units are high in instant damage potential, but costly and quickly falls off.

Losing units is generally painful, and the faction relies on tech, spells, gimmicks to succeed.

Split between casters and automatons. Automatons' health pool consists mostly of a shield, which heals quickly after combat, but is generally fairly compared to health, doesn't get bonuses from defend, etc.

Support units restore shields instead of health, meaning what they get countered fairly easily. No true healing: rechargers restore automaton shields and can't save non-automatons.

Reference: check the "Storm" magic keyword — a lot of combo play, countering enemy plans.

Spells: mostly nuke.

First units (user, 2026-09-25): see `../units/nexus-tier1.md`.

## User direction, 2026-09-26 (canon)
- **Not sci-fi.** "That'd be a mistake." Turrets are probably too much unless clearly arcane.
- **Who they are:** a smaller faction of nobles who do whatever they please, whatever it costs everyone else. They threw a gigantic spirit bomb into the desert just to test their weapons (the origin of Vexumphat).
- **Not real spellcasters:** they studied how magic is done elsewhere and harnessed it through scientific research. Their arcane power is tied to batteries and other equipment; once that's spent, they're spent.
- **The hardest faction to play.**
- **Casters run on spell charges** (converging, not locked): finite charges per fight, restored after combat and rarely otherwise. Charges enhance spells, in the spirit of MTG Izzet's *overload* (pay more for a wider effect: single target becomes "each") and *replicate* (pay again per copy, new targets each). *Storm* doesn't combine well with overload and is dropped. The Apprentice is the simplest case (a limited area spell); later casters offer choices in how to spend. Every caster's default attack stays weak: use momentum, time is never on your side.
