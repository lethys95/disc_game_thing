# Nodes: a brainstorm (Claude's proposals)

> **Picked (user, 2026-09-28):** foundry, leech pits, stables (was kennels), tannery, watchtower, bell tower, ossuary, quarry, siege workshop, menagerie; the ferry became portals (a map structure). With the user's changes, they're canon in `../nodes.md`.

What exists: gold mine, mana node, Blacksmith (the user's: recruits deal +10, for good), Cathedral (the user's: recruits carry Holy Water). A node belongs to the nearest city and levels up with gold.

The Blacksmith and Cathedral found the best pattern this game has: **a recruit mark**. "Where was this unit born" becomes part of the unit's story (the track record). That makes cities different from each other *in the army*, not just in income. Most proposals below extend it. A second pattern is nodes that act on the **map around them**, which makes the map's geography matter.

## Levels should change what a node does
Today a node's levels multiply one number. Proposal: level 1 and 2 raise the number; **level 3 adds a new clause**. The investment becomes a decision ("do I want the capstone here?") rather than a formality.
- Blacksmith 3: recruits also ignore 5 of their target's armor.
- Cathedral 3: Holy Water gets a second charge, or it also removes Punishment and bleed.
- Gold mine 3: the city's garrison is paid from it, so recruiting there costs 20% less.
- Mana node 3: one spell a turn cast *from* this city costs half.

## Recruit-mark nodes ("born here")
- **Training yard**: recruits arrive one level up (not a tier; minor stats). Plain, but it makes one city your officer school. At level 3 they arrive two levels up.
- **Foundry**: recruits carry 10 shield, which regenerates like Nexus shields. It gives shields to factions that have none, a real change to how armor-piercing enemies see them.
- **Witch hut** (the user's example for haunted woods): recruits are **hexed**: whoever kills them is Punished for 2 stacks. Dying costs the enemy something. It pairs with Jilliath's sacrifice and the Wastes' disposable numbers.
- **Leech pits** (marsh): recruits heal for 20% of the damage they deal.
- **Kennels** (forest): recruits give their warband +1 movement on the map while they're in it; this doesn't stack. A fast warband needs one hound-bred unit, so you'll protect it.
- **Tannery** (plains): recruits take 5 less from the first hit of each battle. It's a small mark, but it stacks with armor differently from armor.
- **Scriptorium**: recruits of a caster line start with +1 charge on their limited abilities (the Thaumaturge's lightning, Holy Water). It makes one city the mage academy.

## Map nodes (act around them)
- **Watchtower**: its holder sees 4 hexes around it, through fog. At level 3, enemy warbands seen from it show their true squad (it breaks the Mask of many faces).
- **Ferry, or waystones** (a linked pair on the map): a warband standing on one can step to the other for 1 movement. Whoever holds both cities owns a shortcut; holding one owns half. Fights will happen at them.
- **Toll bridge** (on a river or chasm hex): enemy warbands crossing pay its holder 20 gold each time, or can't cross if they can't pay. It makes the map's chokepoints economic.
- **Waystation**: your warbands that start a turn next to it heal 10%. It gives a front line somewhere to lean on.
- **Bell tower**: when an enemy warband enters the city's radius, you're told; in the first round of a battle at the city, the garrison acts first. An answer to "the AI walked into my city while I was looking elsewhere".
- **Ossuary** (haunted woods, Wastes borders): resurrection in this city is possible without the Capitol research, and 30% cheaper. For Vexumphat it's 60%. It's where your army regrows, so it's what the enemy wants to burn.
- **Quarry** (highlands): this city's upgrades cost half, and its garrison's `fortified` armor is +3. A fortress town.
- **Siege workshop**: warbands leaving this city carry **Battering rams**: the first battle against a city ignores the city's fortification armor. It's the node-side fix for the Capitol stalemates found in M51.

## Nodes that recruit something
The pillars already allow neutral units in garrisons, outside the city's recruit limit.
- **Menagerie / tribal outpost**: this city can recruit one unit type from the biome's tribe (bandits in the plains, whatever lives in the marsh). They're neutral units in your army, with no line to evolve through, but they level past their tier (the D2 rule already in the pillars). It's how tribes become more than obstacles.
- **Mercenary guildhall**: every mercenary camp on the map sells to its holder for 25% less.

## Nodes that care about other nodes
Used sparingly, these make a city's *set* of nodes matter.
- **Aqueduct**: every other node of this city counts one level higher. A city with an aqueduct is worth taking even when its nodes are poor.
- **Shrine** (canon: "map sites that unlock hidden evolution routes"): as a node, it's owned through its city, so holding it keeps the route open. Losing the city closes the route for *new* evolutions (existing units keep theirs). It turns a shrine city into something to defend.

## Faction flavor without faction locks
Any faction can hold any node. Some nodes simply read differently per faction, as the Cathedral does for Jilliath:
- A **Cathedral** in Wastes hands: recruits carry Holy Water that heals the Wastes' shields instead of HP (a spirit in a reliquary).
- A **Foundry** in Nexus hands: recruits' shields also start at +10 over their maximum (overcharge; the Mutant loves it).
- A **Grove-held Leech pit**: the heal comes over 2 turns instead of at once (the Grove heals slowly).

Idea behind this: a node you capture from someone else should feel like *theirs being turned to your use*.

## Round three (Claude, 2026-10-15): level-3 clauses, faction readings, merchants by biome
The user took two ideas without contents on 2026-10-15: "level 3 adds a clause" and "a node reads differently per
faction" ("as long as nodes aren't stubs for other factions"), and said merchants and mercenary camps differ by biome
(camps are built). Proposals for picking, on the phone page `shots/city-nodes-2.html`.

### Level-3 clauses (one per node)
- **Gold mine:** plunder: whoever captures the city takes a few turns of the mine's gold. A rich city becomes a target.
- **Mana node:** your first spell each turn aimed near the city costs half. Fights get pulled toward it.
- **Blacksmith:** recruits' attacks ignore part of their target's armor.
- **Cathedral:** Holy Water also cleanses: bleeding, burning, venom and Punishment leave its target.
- **Foundry:** healing that would overfill the bearer's health goes into its plating.
- **Leech pits:** a kill heals the bearer as much as the killing blow dealt.
- **Stables:** pursuit: a warband with a stable-bred unit that wins a battle it started keeps its movement left.
- **Tannery:** it softens the first hit of every round, not only the battle's first.
- **Siege workshop:** a warband with a siege-trained unit standing next to an enemy city lowers its walls while it stays.
- **Quarry:** the city's walls can't be lowered by spells (Break walls).
- **Ossuary:** units raised here come back a level up.
- **Watchtower:** it also shows the items of the warbands it sees, and where each last marched.
- **Bell tower:** when the bells ring, your nearest warband within a few hexes gets extra movement next turn.
- **Tribal outpost:** a tribe unit recruited here can be elevated to lead a warband.
- **Venom distillery:** every hit on an already envenomed target adds a dose, not only the first.
- **Witch hut:** the curse passes on: the killer is hexed in turn for the rest of the battle.
- **Hunting lodge:** trophies: a warband with a hunter earns gold for each neutral it kills.
- **Training yard:** units resting in its garrison gain a little XP each turn.
- **Gallows:** a warband wiped out at the city leaves its leader's items to the city's holder.
- **Waystation:** your warbands that start a turn next to it also get +1 movement that turn.
- **Caravanserai:** you see every merchant's wares and every camp's hires from anywhere.
- **Bog iron:** enemy units fighting at the city have less armor (the bog eats iron).
- **Granary:** provisions: your warbands leaving the city heal as if resting for two more turns.
- **Oasis:** enemy warbands within a couple of hexes of the city heal nothing.
- **Dog yard:** enemy warbands ending a march within its reach are shown to you for a turn, through fog.
- **Waystone:** Way portal can bring any of your warbands, not only those elevated here.

### Faction readings (each reading a real effect, not a stub)
- **Cathedral:** Jilliath: Holy Water (as now). Ral-Vitahl: a charge cell restores an ally's shield once per battle (no true healing, as the faction). Grove: sap heals an ally over a few turns, more than Holy Water in all.
- **Foundry:** Jilliath: heals that reach the bearer mend its plating too. Ral-Vitahl: overcharge: the plating starts above its maximum. Grove: bark: the plating never mends, but the bearer heals each turn while any of it holds.
- **Leech pits:** Jilliath: the leeched health goes to the ally beside it with the least health (health as a resource). Ral-Vitahl: it restores shield instead of health. Grove: the heal comes over two turns, and more of it.
- **Ossuary:** Jilliath: raised units come back hexed (their killer is Punished). Ral-Vitahl: automatons are rebuilt here at half price. Grove: raised units come back with a heal over time in their first battle.
- **Witch hut:** Jilliath: the killer is Punished (as now). Ral-Vitahl: the killer is discharged, its shield gone. Grove: the killer withers.
- **Scriptorium** (the user's maybe, waiting for exactly this): Jilliath: casters' first use of their own health as a resource each battle costs none. Ral-Vitahl: casters start each battle with an extra spell charge. Grove: casters' heals over time last a turn longer.
