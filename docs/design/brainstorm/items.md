# Items: a brainstorm (Claude's proposals)

Slots today: head, armor, weapon, two utility, banner, and a bag. The user's Stratholme test: an item is memorable when it does something no other item does, and when it rewards knowing it well (Rivendare's sword is stronger in the right hands).

Effort tags: **[fits]** the effect system already does it, or nearly; **[hook]** needs a new hook point in the rules; **[system]** needs something new (item memory, map rules, a new kind of thing).

## 1. Items that remember
Items with a history make the stories people retell. An item carries a small counter that survives being worn by someone else, sold or taken as spoils.

- **Grudgebearer** (weapon). +3 damage for every enemy *leader* it has killed, forever. Its tooltip lists them by name ("Took: Leader of the red warband, turn 14"). A sword taken from a dead rival carries that rival's tally and their victims. **[system]** - lethys: we can add this, but I'm afraid the name list could end up as spam if we're not careful.
- **Glass cuirass** (armor). +20 armor, and it breaks after absorbing 12 hits *in total, across battles*. It becomes "Glass shards" in the bag, which sell for a little. A spending resource shaped like armor: save it for the fight that matters, or take it off. **[system]** - i personally find temporary equipment frustrating. players skip these in practice
- **The debt-collector's ledger** (utility). Each battle the warband *loses* (retreats from or wins without the leader) adds a mark. At 3 marks its next battle starts with every enemy Punished by 1 stack. Losing slowly builds a comeback. **[system]** - lethys: i think its use is too obscure anf inconsistent for players to want to pick it
- **Veteran's standard** (banner). +1 damage to the warband for every battle this banner has been through, capped at +10. A new banner is useless and an old one is precious. Losing it as spoils hurts. **[system]** - lethys: 10 damage is not much. maybe buff this, but otherwise ok. please add

## 2. Items with a drawback (curses)
The drawback makes you build around the item, and that's what you remember.

- **Hungering blade** (weapon). +15 damage. It can't be taken off. Each turn its warband doesn't fight, every unit in it loses 5 HP. It forces aggression, and doubles as a weapon against the cold-war stalemates the sims keep finding. **[hook]** - lethys: I'm generally not a fan of cursed items. In practice people just pick consistent items. so we can add this, but passive regeneratiom would heal everything back anyway between turns. It's not much of a punishment.
- **Crown of the pretender** (head). +2 Leadership (up to the grid's 9). The warband can never retreat, and the leader can never Defend. **[fits]** lethys: leadership is arguably the most powerful stat in the game. I'd be careful distributing it loosely. I think 1 leadership extra is enough, otherwise a lucky drop will just win you the game. speaking of luck... maybe the player should be able to see the loot in dungeons beforehand to fit our no rng theme.
- **Martyr's shroud** (armor, Jilliath resonance). The first time the leader would die in a battle, the ally orthogonally next to it with the most HP dies instead. If that ally is Jilliath, the leader also heals to full. Sacrifice as a mechanic, and grim enough to remember. **[hook]** - lethys: I"m not a fan of faction specific items. they're not fun to get unless you're playing the faction. this also scales poorly if we were to add more factions. I think I'm saying pass on this one
- **Penitent's chains** (armor). The leader takes half of all damage aimed at orthogonally adjacent allies, and gains +10 armor. It turns any leader into a bodyguard, so where you place them in the grid becomes the item. **[hook]** - Ok, but don't make it cursed. this is just a good item.
- **Ossuary lantern** (utility). Units of this warband that die don't go to the graveyard; they're gone for good. In exchange, every battle the warband wins pays gold equal to the XP it earned. A Vexumphat player would never wear it, and a Nexus speed-runner might. **[hook]** - lethys: pass. graveyard is completely core, gold isnt precious enough. nobody would pick this

## 3. Items that bend the grid
Positions, rows and turn order are the core of this game's combat; items should play with them.

- **Pendulum flail** (weapon). The leader's attacks alternate: the first is +15 damage, the next +0, and so on. With initiative giving multiple actions, which pass the big swing lands on becomes the puzzle; Wait lines it up. **[hook]** - lethys: pass. the mental overhead of keeping track becomes annoying. 
- **Twin knives** (weapon). The leader's attack hits twice at 60% each. Flat armor applies to each hit, so they're bad against Congregant plate and superb against shields and unarmored casters. It's the armor-versus-shield pillar as a tool you can hold. **[fits]** - lethys: this might get too strong wilh abilities that apply effects, but okay. i accept. no picking different targets though. same target
- **Reaching spear** (weapon). The leader's melee reaches the enemy's *middle* row, but its damage there is halved. The first melee that can pick a caster out from behind a wall, at a price. **[fits]**
- **Banner of the vanguard** (banner). In the first pass of each round, the warband's front row acts before anyone else, whatever the initiative. It breaks the Nexus casters' alpha strike. **[hook]**
- **Hourglass of borrowed time** (utility). Once per battle, as a free action: the leader takes its next action *now*, and skips its first action of the next round. Tempo shifted, not gained. **[hook]**
- **Mirror shield** (armor). The first ability that targets *only* the leader in each battle is turned back on its caster. A Justiciar-style cancel for anyone, deterministic and readable. **[hook]**

## 4. Items that play on the map
Leaders walk the map; half their life is there. The map has fog, remembered sightings, movement, merchants and spells.

- **Mask of many faces** (head). In the fog, and on enemy hover forecasts, this warband shows as a weaker squad: its three lowest-tier units, and the leader. The enemy AI believes the lie too. The first deterministic *bluff* in the game. **[system]**
- **Deed to a silver mine** (utility). Its leader earns 30 gold per turn. If the warband is wiped out, the victor takes the deed (spoils). It paints a gold target on your back, and the enemy can see who carries it (a crest over the warband). **[fits]**
- **Wayfinder's map** (utility). Forest and hills cost this warband what plains cost. Dull on paper, but it decides whole flanking routes. **[fits]**
- **Twin compasses** (utility, found as a pair). Two leaders each carrying one can swap places on the map once per turn, anywhere. Elevation is free, so a cheap leader at the front plus a strong one at home becomes a teleporting defense. **[system]**
- **Lodestone** (utility). Enemy warbands that start their turn next to this one can't move away. It pins a runner; zone of control as an item. **[hook]**
- **Spyglass helm** (head). +2 sight on the map. Filler, but the kind every map game needs. **[fits]**
- **Salt of the old kings** (consumable). Sprinkled on a hex next to the warband: nothing can enter that hex for 3 turns. It blocks a bridge or a pass, and it's lore-able against the Wastes (salt wards spirits). **[system]**
- **Smoke powder** (consumable). Retreat from a battle before it starts, with no losses and no XP given away. A cheap answer to a miscalculated forecast. **[fits]**

## 5. Faction resonance (the Rivendare trick)
Anyone can use these items; one faction or unit type gets more from them. That rewards knowing the game and makes spoils feel different depending on who picks them up.

- **Tithe knife** (weapon). +5 damage for each ally that dies in the battle so far. On a Jilliath leader it's also +5 for each ally that *lost HP to its own ability* (Fanaticism, Martyrdom). **[hook]**
- **Storm capacitor** (utility). Once per battle: restore 20 shield to every unit that has shield. Anyone can use it; for a Nexus squad it's a full recharge, and for a Mutant it triggers Mutate (a full shield restored). **[fits]**
- **Burial wrappings** (armor). The leader has a Death ward (it stops at 1 HP, once per battle, the user's idea for the Wastes). A Vexumphat leader instead gives the ward to its whole front row. **[fits]**
- **Seedpod reliquary** (utility). At the start of each of the leader's turns, the ally with the lowest HP heals 5. For a Grove leader it's 10, lasting 2 turns after the leader falls (a heal-over-time, the Grove's healing identity). **[fits]**

## 6. Items that change the warband itself
- **Hollow plate** (armor, Wastes resonance). It isn't worn: it stands in a free tile of the grid as a unit. That's 60 HP, 10 armor, a weak attack, and no XP. If it falls, the item drops to the bag, dented, and is restored at a city. "Animated armor" from the Wastes' own notes, as loot. **[system]**
- **War drum** (banner). The warband's total actions per round, summed across units, can't drop below its count at battle start. A unit that loses initiative to Punishment keeps its actions. A direct counter to Jilliath's Punisher, and a readable one. **[hook]**
- **Mercenary's writ** (utility). The leader may hire from any mercenary camp it can *see*, without walking there, at +50% price. **[fits]**
- **Recruiter's cap** (head). After a won battle against neutrals, the leader may hire one defeated neutral unit at its mercenary price (D2 allowed rare neutral recruitment; the pillars mention it). **[hook]**

## 7. Consumables worth carrying
- **Bottled lightning**. At the start of the next battle: 20 damage to every enemy in the back row. It snipes the casters. **[fits]**
- **Oil of sundering**. For the next battle, every hit from the warband removes 2 armor from its target, stacking. **[fits]** (the `sundered` effect exists)
- **Letter of passage**. The warband can cross a neutral city's hexes without fighting, once. **[hook]**
- **Scroll of recall**. The warband returns to its Capitol. It costs the rest of the leader's movement and next turn's. **[fits]**
- **Relic of a dead saint** (Jilliath resonance). Used in a battle on a fallen ally, it revives that ally at 25% HP. On a Jilliath leader, the ally revives at 50% HP, and the leader loses 25% of its own. **[hook]**

## A few meta ideas
- **Where it drops says what it is.** Without RNG, rarity can come from *place*: a dungeon's depth, its biome, a tribe's hoard. Haunted woods drop curses; the Nexus-scarred storm fields drop capacitors. A player learns the map's loot, not a drop table.
- **Siege items** (Tattered city banner, Ram-head helm: +X against a city's garrison, ignore the `fortified` armor). The M51 finding was that big-map stalemates are fortress Capitols. Items, nodes and spells aimed at walls are a natural fix that needs no AI cleverness.
- **Sets? No.** Sets reward hoarding and dilute slots. Pairs, like the Twin compasses, give the same "find the other one" pull, and only on purpose.
- **Items as spoils already work** (M35). Remembering items (section 1) make spoils personal: "that's *my* banner he's carrying".
