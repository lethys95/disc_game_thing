## What stands out
1. **The attacker almost always wins (90–97% of battles).** The map AI only attacks when its forecast says it wins,
   so nearly every battle is a mop-up, not a contest. Games are decided by *how often* a side attacks, not by close
   fights. Real players will take riskier fights, but it means the AI never forces a hard decision on the player
   ("ultimatums", Jilliath's identity, can't show in AI play).
2. **Ral-Vitahl beats Jilliath 4 to 1 because it attacks far more:** about 210–225 attacks per six games against
   Jilliath's 77–102, with similar win rates per battle. Either the forecast rates Nexus squads too highly (burst
   damage looks decisive on paper), or Jilliath's squads look too weak to the AI to risk fights. In the composition
   sims Jilliath's best squads are as strong as Nexus's, so the AI's judgement is the first suspect, not the units.
3. **Whole systems barely appear.** Per turn, per player: learning spells ~0.01–0.02 (one or two per game), hiring
   mercenaries ~0.01, investing in nodes ~0.02, upgrading cities ~0.01, buying items ~0.03. The Sylvan casts almost no
   spells (0.01–0.03 a turn). Either the AI doesn't value them, or they cost too much for what they give. Most of the
   economy's breadth is invisible in play.
4. **Gold piles up with nothing to buy.** Long games end with 6,000–7,000 unspent gold per side (Huge maps, cold
   wars). Past the mid-game there's no sink, and both sides' Capitols become untakeable (`provisional.md`: Guardian
   plus eight defenders behind upgraded walls).
5. **Churn:** around 0.25–0.4 resurrections and 0.1–0.2 revivals per turn per player, on top of ~1 recruit a turn.
   Armies are rebuilt constantly; losses rarely stick.
6. **The Sylvan holds its own** against both (they've never played whole games before): even with Jilliath and
   Ral-Vitahl, best in the three-way. Its games are short: it finishes, or is finished, around turn 45.

## Suggested fixes (the user picks)
- **A. The AI's appetite (2):** compare the battle forecast to actual outcomes per faction; if Nexus is over-rated or
  Jilliath under-rated, fix the forecast. Cheap, and it fixes Jilliath's whole-game record without touching units.
- **B. Make the dead systems worth it (3):** for each (spells, mercenaries, nodes, city upgrades, items), check
  whether the AI ignores it or the numbers make it a bad buy (`sim:world` with one system forced on, compared with
  off). Then teach the AI, or change the price or payoff.
- **C. A late-game gold sink and a way into fortified Capitols (4):** decisions for the user. The existing knobs are
  garrison slots or wall armor; or something to spend on (higher tiers, spells, a siege option).
- **D. Contested fights (1):** let the AI take even fights when it's ahead overall, or when a target is worth it
  (a Capitol, a node), so battles aren't all foregone. Needs care: it can make the AI reckless.
- **E. Churn (5):** if losses should matter more, resurrection cost or a limit per turn. The user's call (Jilliath's
  identity leans on resurrection).

## Fix A, done: the forecast was never wrong; Jilliath's opening squad was
- The map AI's forecast isn't an estimate: battles are deterministic, and the AI plays each candidate fight out in
  full before choosing. So "the forecast misjudges Jilliath" was the wrong suspect.
- `scripts/balance/openings.ts` puts each faction's opening squad against every tribe's groups and the other openings:
  - **Jilliath started with five Congregants,** two of them in the back row, where melee can't reach. It lost to
    every other opening and won medium camps with 20–37% of its health left. Ral-Vitahl and Sylvan start with three
    melee, a support and a mage.
  - With **three Congregants, a Cleric and a mage** (its other tier-1 formation, now its opening), Jilliath beats both
    other openings and keeps 43–70% of its health on medium camps.
- **Whole games after the change** (8 seeds, both orders):

  | Pairing | Before | After |
  |---|---|---|
  | Jilliath vs Ral-Vitahl | 3–12 (1 cold war) | 6–9 (1 cold war) |
  | Jilliath vs Sylvan | 7–9 | 6–10 |

  Within the noise of 16 games against the Sylvan, Ral-Vitahl still slightly ahead. Games are shorter too.
- **Also seen:** in a mirror match of openings, the attacker lost for Jilliath and Ral-Vitahl (and won for Sylvan).
  Equal squads may favour the defender's seat; worth a look if it shows up elsewhere.
- Provisional #70.

## Fix B: the systems aren't ignored, they run out
- `VERBOSE=1 pnpm sim:world` now prints each game's end state: node levels, city tiers, spells learned. In long games
  the AI buys nearly everything there is:
  - every gold mine at level 3 and almost every city's walls at tier 4;
  - every spell it can learn;
  - dozens of items.

  The low per-turn rates in the first pass come from early scarcity (gold goes to units first) and from there being
  little to buy.
- **One real AI gap, fixed:** the AI only invested in gold mines. Mana nodes and the ten special kinds (Blacksmith,
  Foundry, Quarry, Watchtower, Bell tower and the rest) stayed at level 1 all game. It now raises them once the mines
  are done: mana next, then a node's gifts to its city, then the recruit marks of nodes at its Capitol, where it
  recruits. In the re-run (32 games) the cold wars were gone (the two seeds that stalled won on turns 136 and 140);
  wins otherwise much as before.
- **What's left is content, the user's to design:**
  - Jilliath and Ral-Vitahl have two spells each, the Sylvan none (only the two neutral ones from mage towers;
    questions #7);
  - the items are placeholders (#8);
  - nothing costs gold past the mid-game (C).
