# Gothic RPG / action-RPG HUDs — reference notes

Games covered: Path of Exile 1 and 2, Grim Dawn, Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption, Arcanum,
Divine Divinity, Blasphemous 1 and 2, Dungeon Keeper 1 and 2, Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain, plus one
Bloodborne menu as a counterpoint.

40 screenshots plus 5 `-detail` crops (zoomed HUD corners cut from the kept screenshots). Sources:
Steam store screenshots and Bing image search results. Some older-game images are upscales or
YouTube frames, so they're soft. `poe2-passive-tree-1.jpg` carries a TheGamer watermark.
`poe1-tooltip-rare-1.jpg` was cropped to remove a third-party overlay.

Everything below describes what is visible in the images. Anything added from memory is marked
"(from memory, not in the screenshots)".

---

### Path of Exile 1 — life globe held by a statue
- Image: `poe1-hud-globes-flasks-1.jpg` (bottom left); zoom in `poe1-hud-corners-detail.jpg` (top half)
- What's there: a red liquid sphere about 1/9 of the screen height. A dark bronze statue of a seated, crowned woman sits on its left side. She leans against the globe and her arm and spear cross in front of its lower edge. The globe's rim is a thin bronze ring that runs into a scroll volute at the bottom, and the volute becomes the start of the bottom bar.
- What works: the figure has a job, holding up the globe. Her body overlaps the rim, so you can't read globe and statue as two pasted layers. Her spear crosses the frame line, the strongest single trick for making a figure feel built in. The bronze matches the bar's metal and catches the same warm light.
- What doesn't: at small sizes the statue is a dark silhouette and only its outline reads.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, unit card): put a Jilliath angel behind the unit-card portrait and let her wing or sword cross *in front of* the frame edge, so she holds the card instead of standing beside it. The overlap is what removes the "bolted on" look.

### Path of Exile 1 — mana globe with a second, different figure
- Image: `poe1-hud-globes-flasks-1.jpg` (bottom right); `poe1-hud-corners-detail.jpg` (bottom half)
- What's there: the mana globe is dark slate with a raised relief texture rather than plain liquid (a status here, from memory). A helmeted woman leans over its right shoulder, one arm draped over the rim. A wreath-like bronze rim wraps the lower half.
- What works: the two corner statues are different people in different poses (sitting and holding vs. leaning and embracing), even though they're one sculptor's family. Mirror symmetry would have made them read as copies.
- Idea for disc (overworld): the turn bar's two ends could carry two different carved figures from the faction in play (an inquisitor and an angel; a noble and a battery-keeper), never mirrored.

### Path of Exile 1 — the flask belt
- Image: `poe1-hud-globes-flasks-1.jpg` (bottom left, right of the globe); `poe1-hud-corners-detail.jpg`
- What's there: five flasks stand in bays under small round arches set on thin pilasters, like a reliquary shelf. Each flask is a detailed object (gold lantern, green bulb with a sigil) whose liquid level shows its charges. Key numbers 1–5 sit under each bay. "MENU" sits on a small carved plaque with a pediment above it.
- What works: the slots are architecture (arches and columns), not boxes. The objects are shown, not represented by icons.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, ability bar): set the ability buttons in a small arcade, a row of pointed arches on columns, with each faction's arch shape different (Jilliath lancet, Ral-Vitahl ogee, Sylvan arches made of bent branches). The arch row *is* the bar, so nothing has to be stuck onto it.

### Path of Exile 2 — globe with a segmented outer ring and a figure wrestling a serpent
- Image: `poe2-hud-globes-1.jpg`, `poe2-hud-corners-detail.jpg`
- What's there: the red life globe is ringed by a band of pale turquoise segments that look like enamel tiles, a second resource drawn around the first (energy shield, from memory). On the left, a muscular bronze male figure is coiled with a serpent-like form whose tail runs down into the frame. The blue mana globe on the right has a gold segmented band and a cloaked figure. At the inner end of the left bar stands a small bronze statuette holding up a hoop, acting as the bar's end post.
- What works: the ring segments let one round element show two values without a second widget. The statuette as an end post has a structural job: it ends the rail.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, unit card): show hit points as the inner liquid or fill and armour or ward as a segmented outer ring of tiles, which suits a pale-marble ring for Jilliath. Use small figures as end posts on rails (the turn-order strip, the turn bar) instead of the same angel at every corner.

### Path of Exile 2 — temple console (a planning board as a carved object)
- Image: `poe2-temple-screen-1.jpg`
- What's there: a full-screen planning board drawn as a diamond-shaped stone slab with raised, studded stone walls, viewed at a slight 3D angle. Placed rooms are gold-outlined tiles; available spots glow faint green. On the left is a list of room types: dark plaques with small-caps names, each ending in a gold-rimmed lozenge medallion holding an icon, with the selected one glowing gold. At the top a title plaque, "Temple Console", sits in a bronze cartouche. At the bottom centre, "Run Temple" is a wide copper button on a pedestal that grows out of the HUD frame, crowned with a row of small gold crenellations.
- What works: the commit button is the only warm, copper object on a cold stone screen, so you can't miss it. The list's medallions repeat the board's diamond geometry, which ties the two halves together.
- Idea for disc (overworld end-turn button, capitol build screen): make end turn a heavier object that physically grows out of the frame on a pedestal, the one warm-metal element on the screen. For the capitol, consider a small carved board of the city's districts, with each build option a plaque ending in a medallion.

### Path of Exile 2 — inventory set into a grisaille relief
- Image: `poe2-inventory-statues-1.jpg`
- What's there: the equipment area's background is a painted grey-stone relief. Two crowned skeletal kings with gold halos sit enthroned left and right, a crowned woman holds a spear at lower right, and a helmeted figure stands before flames at lower left. The item slots are dark rectangular windows cut *into* the relief. The crest above shows two winged griffin or lion figures flanking a hooded, glowing figure over a gold "Inventory" plaque. Below is a plain dark grid with a quatrefoil pattern in each cell.
- What works: the sculpture is the wall the slots are cut into, never a decoration on top of the frame. That is the cleanest answer in the set to the user's problem. The only colour in the relief is the gold halos, so the items stay the brightest things.
- Idea for disc (warband panel, capitol): instead of angels at the panel corners, make the panel's background a low-contrast carved relief (a Jilliath tribunal of angels, a Ral-Vitahl banquet, a Sylvan grove of root-bodies) and cut the unit slots and stat plaques into it. Keep it near-grisaille with one accent colour.

### Path of Exile 1 — inventory crest and kneeling stone figures
- Image: `poe1-inventory-1.jpg` (right half)
- What's there: a gilded acanthus crest with a small round medallion of crossed tools sits above a gold "Inventory" plaque. In the equipment area, two stone statues kneel left and right behind the glove and boot slots, half hidden in shadow. The slots are deep navy, and the flask row sits on a dark pedestal under the armour slot.
- What works: the statues are dim and partly covered by slots, so they read as depth rather than decoration.
- What doesn't: the bright navy slot fill is a flat digital colour that fights the bronze.
- Idea for disc (codex): darken and partly cover the carved figures, letting the content overlap them. A figure that is half behind a plaque reads as built in.

### Path of Exile — tooltip header by rarity
- Image: `poe1-tooltip-rare-1.jpg` (rare: gold), `poe1-tooltip-unique-1.jpg` (unique: copper and orange)
- What's there: a two-line name plate across the top. Rare has a dark-olive band with gold small-caps text and gold acanthus ornaments at both ends. Unique has a brown-orange band with orange text and darker ornaments. The body is near-black and semi-transparent. Lines are centred small caps, separated by short thin rules that fade at both ends. Values are blue for magic mods and white for base numbers, and the lore line is orange italic.
- What works: rarity lives in the header colour and its end ornaments, not in a border. The body stays identical, so tooltips read as one family. The fading rules add elegance without being frames.
- Idea for disc (unit card and codex): vary the header plate by unit tier or faction (marble plate for Jilliath, lacquer-and-brass for Ral-Vitahl, a bark or horn plate for Sylvan) over one shared dark body.

### Path of Exile 2 — vendor window: cell colour as state, NPC rising behind
- Image: `poe2-vendor-tooltip-1.jpg`
- What's there: in the vendor grid each item sits on a coloured cell: deep red for items you can't use, green for one, blue for the rest. The tooltip's header has gold scroll ornaments. Keywords ("Physical Damage", "Stun Buildup") carry a dotted underline. The NPC's head and shoulders rise *behind* the tooltip's top edge, and a quatrefoil gothic crest crowns the "Buy or Sell" plaque.
- What works: you read the state from the cell's colour before reading text. The dotted underline promises a definition without cluttering the line. The portrait behind the frame edge makes the window feel placed in the world.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, battle log and unit card): underline rules terms in ability text with a dotted line and show their definition on hover. Tint grid cells on the 3x3 battle grids by state (can't act, targeted, guarded) with a muted fill rather than outlines.

### Path of Exile 1 — stash crest and custom slot layouts
- Image: `poe1-stash-1.jpg`
- What's there: a large gold acanthus crest whose central medallion holds a chest icon, over a gold "Stash" plaque, with a red round close button. The stash tab is a teal engraved damask panel with a symmetrical pattern of fixed slots and a tall empty slot in the centre, each slot meant for one item type. User-coloured tabs (orange, tan, blue, purple, magenta) run along the top, and a dropdown list repeats them.
- What works: a fixed, symmetrical slot pattern turns storage into a display cabinet, and empty slots show what's missing.
- What doesn't: the user-coloured tabs are saturated flat colours that break the material palette.
- Idea for disc (capitol): show the capitol's buildings or upgrades as a fixed, symmetrical cabinet of slots (unbuilt ones empty and carved), not a list. That fits "pale marble fact plaques" laid out like a reliquary wall.

### Path of Exile 2 — passive tree centre portrait
- Image: `poe2-passive-tree-1.jpg` (centre)
- What's there: a large circular painting of the class sits in a thin dark ring with small quatrefoil studs at the four compass points. Thin node connections radiate from those studs, and the allocated path glows gold. The rest of the screen is node webs on a near-black field.
- What works: the frame's studs are where the tree's lines start, so the ornament is a functional junction.
- Idea for disc (codex or faction screen): a round painted medallion of the faction where the frame's studs become the start points of tech or ability lines.

### Path of Exile 1 — ascendancy sub-tree drawn over the painting
- Image: `poe1-ascendancy-1.jpg`
- What's there: a circular painted vignette of the class with its own small node tree drawn *over* the painting, and a flavour quote in white italic at the bottom of the circle. A large round medallion docks on the circle's rim at the right, holding three coloured counters (blue, red, green) around a sculpted face. Gold buttons sit in a top bar: "1 Point Left", "Apply Refunds", "Cancel".
- What works: painting, data and ornament occupy the same disc. The medallion bolted to the rim looks deliberately attached, like a clasp.
- Idea for disc (new-game screen): when choosing a faction, show its painting in a large roundel with the faction's few key traits drawn as nodes over the image, plus one line of lore in italics.

### Grim Dawn — health and energy as glass tubes in brass housings
- Image: `gd-hud-1.jpg` (bottom centre); also visible in `gd-inventory-tooltip-1.jpg`
- What's there: two long horizontal glass tubes, red for health and green for energy, each about 1/5 of the screen wide. They're filled with swirling liquid and have highlight streaks, held by riveted brass clamps at top and bottom. Between them, a round iron boss shows the level number in gold. Below runs a thin gold XP rail, and a row of square skill icons underneath. Iron scrollwork flourishes curl out at both outer ends.
- What works: the bars are glass vessels clamped into a frame, so the frame holds them physically, with clamps. Material and lighting are consistent: specular streaks on the glass and warm brass.
- Idea for disc (Ral-Vitahl, arcane batteries): the Ral-Vitahl HUD could show mana or charge as glass battery tubes clamped in brass, with a central iron boss for the turn number. That ties faction lore to UI structure.

### Grim Dawn — round minimap and area name
- Image: `gd-hud-1.jpg` (top right)
- What's there: a circular minimap in a thick iron ring with a small notch at north and a red marker at south. The area name, "Burrwitch Outskirts", sits above it in pale blackletter-style text with no plaque behind it.
- What works: blackletter used only for place names gives places their own voice.
- Idea for disc (overworld): name hexes and regions in a separate display typeface used only for places, and keep the body face for numbers.

### Grim Dawn — side-by-side comparison tooltips and a merchant plaque
- Image: `gd-inventory-tooltip-1.jpg`
- What's there: two tooltips side by side, "Item for Sale" (green rare name) and "Currently Equipped" (blue epic name, with a gold item name for a component). The merchant panel's top is an iron cartouche with a round crest medallion and a two-line plaque reading "Luther Graves — Salvage Dealer". Vendor grid cells are tinted yellow, red or green behind the items. The character panel uses grey stone tabs, "CHAR I / II / III".
- What works: showing the comparison by default prevents mistakes. The merchant's name plaque gives the shop a person.
- What doesn't: the tooltips are plain dark boxes with no frame, a sharp drop in craft next to the iron windows.
- Idea for disc (capitol): give each building or vendor in the capitol a name plaque with a small crest medallion. When a unit upgrade is offered, show "current" and "after" cards side by side.

### Grim Dawn — Devotion: a skill tree as constellations
- Image: `gd-devotion-1.jpg`
- What's there: a full-screen nebula sky, purple and blue in the centre, green at lower left, orange at upper right. Each constellation is a pale line drawing of a creature or object (scales, owl, crane, scorpion, throne, hammer and anvil, a bat) with stars at the joints. Lit stars glow gold and unlit ones pale blue. The left side has an "Affinities" panel with five coloured gem icons and counts, framed in dark metal with a torn, irregular edge.
- What works: every node belongs to a picture, so the screen is beautiful and still readable. Colour regions in the nebula hint at the affinities.
- Idea for disc (codex, or spells and abilities screen): for the Sylvan, draw abilities as nodes on a drawing of a tree or root system, where the nodes are buds or knots and the "lit" state is new growth. For Jilliath, use stained-glass leading lines. A different picture per faction gives the screens a family structure with different faces.

### Grim Dawn — mastery tree with a level rail
- Image: `gd-mastery-tree-1.jpg`
- What's there: a painted class card on the left in a thin frame. Skill nodes form horizontal rows linked by thin gold lines. At the bottom runs a long purple fill bar inside a riveted iron rail with tick plaques at thresholds (1, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 32, 40, 50), and faint vertical purple lines rise from each tick through the tree. "Points Available" sits in red at the left end, beside a round purple plus button.

### Grim Dawn — world map as a painted chart with a scroll banner
- Image: `gd-world-map-1.jpg`
- What's there: a painted map (ochre lands, teal sea) inside an iron frame. The title "Cairn" sits on a parchment scroll banner with curled ends, and thin sunburst lines radiate from it over the sea. Place names are in white outlined small caps. Red spiral icons mark rift gates and yellow ticks mark completed areas. On the right edge, a vertical gilded rod with art-nouveau tracery ends in lily-like finials, like a map's hanging rod or clasp.

- Idea for disc (overworld): the side panels could hang from a single gilded rod with finials, like a map hanging from its rod, instead of being boxes with corner ornaments. Give a region-name banner a curled scroll.

### Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption — the blood-soaked bottom frame
- Image: `vtmr-hud-party-1.jpg`, `vtmr-hud-bar-detail.jpg`
- What's there: a full-width bottom bar about 1/4 of the screen tall. It's built from pale pewter-grey stone or metal with rounded bevels, and its lower edge is soaked in **red blood that pools and drips** across the whole bottom. The left cluster holds three stance buttons on a rounded plate (shield, half moon, sword), an oval cartouche with a four-arrow movement compass, and square ivory buttons with engraved black icons (helmet, sword and shield, cross, eye, compass star). The centre holds a black text window above a row of party portraits, each with a thin blood bar and a green gem. The right cluster holds a row of five red-glass blood vials in square sockets and round enamel discipline medallions with white glyphs: one large medallion for the active discipline and five smaller ones.
- What works: the blood isn't a decoration *on* the frame, it's a substance staining the frame, so it reads as part of the material. The vials make the vampire's resource physical. Each button family has its own shape: round for stance, square for commands, round enamel for powers.
- What doesn't: the pale grey reads cheap next to the dark game view, and the portrait well is a flat black box.
- Idea for disc (Sylvan): instead of carved roots stuck onto the frame, let **rot or moss and sap stain and grow over the frame's lower edge** as a material effect, the way the blood pools here. Also give each button family its own shape.

### Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption — mourner statues framing the inventory
- Image: `vtmr-inventory-2.jpg` (right half)
- What's there: the inventory panel sits in a stone-and-iron frame whose sides are tall **hooded mourner figures** carved in grey stone, standing full height left and right of the equipment area. Slots are deep teal-green with fine patterning. A gold coin icon plaque shows "33340". The grid below has a faint mural painted behind it.
- What works: the figures are the frame's side posts, as tall as the panel, carrying it like caryatids. A full-height figure works as a column, not a corner sticker.
- Idea for disc (warband and city side panels): replace the corner angels with **full-height angel caryatids as the left and right posts** of the panel, with wings folded down the sides and head under the top rail. Then the angel carries the top rail instead of perching on it.

### Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption — character sheet with dot ratings and statues
- Image: `vtmr-character-sheet-1.jpg`
- What's there: the left half holds dark stat panels with blue fill bars and number pairs, headed by small embossed labels ("status", "attributes", "attack", "soak"). In the centre is a small wheel of damage types (fire, bash, lethal, aggravated) with numbers at its spokes. The right half lists disciplines (Heal, Prayer, True Faith) as long plaques, each followed by **five red blood-dot ratings**. Hooded grey stone figures flank the right panel, and red curtains show behind.

- Idea for disc (unit card, codex): show abilities' ranks or tiers as small carved pips, such as blood drops for Ral-Vitahl, seeds for the Sylvan and small wax seals for Jilliath, instead of numbers.

### Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption — main menus set in church architecture
- Image: `vtmr-main-menu-1.jpg`, `vtmr-main-menu-2.jpg`
- What's there: in menu 1, a carved stone arch with a praying hooded statue in the lower left corner frames a red stained-glass window with dark leading. The menu words float over the glass in a lowercase uncial-style face. In menu 2, a red stone ankh cross stands in an arched niche, flanked by stone pilasters carved with emblems (a crown, a rose, a spiral, a wolf, a cross, a mask). The hovered item turns red.

### Arcanum — brass-and-glass gauges and a wood desk HUD
- Image: `arcanum-hud-1.jpg`, `arcanum-character-1.jpg`, `arcanum-inventory-1.jpg`
- What's there: a dark wooden board with brass fittings. Health (red) and fatigue (blue) are vertical glass tubes in brass caps at both ends, with numeric readouts on small brass plaques below. Buttons are round brass-rimmed discs (shield, key, gear, swirl). The character screen adds a **pressure-gauge dial** (labelled E&G, reading "-59") for alignment and a **vertical thermometer** on the right running from a magic symbol at the top to a gear at the bottom (tech vs. magic aptitude). A painted sky window at the top right shows day or night with moon and stars, and a small hourglass sits beside it.
- What works: each value has its own instrument (tube, dial, thermometer, sky window), so you learn them by shape. The material set is coherent: wood, brass and glass everywhere.
- Idea for disc (Ral-Vitahl overworld): one bespoke instrument per resource, such as a battery charge thermometer and a corruption or favour dial. For the turn bar, add a small painted sky window that shows the day or turn phase.

### Arcanum — main menu as a still life on a desk
- Image: `arcanum-main-menu-1.jpg`
- What's there: a 3D-rendered dark desk holding a Tiffany rose lamp, a clockwork box, a flintlock pistol, a pocket watch, a skull with a spider on it, a blue glass orb on a serpent stand and a dagger. In the centre stands a carved wood panel with a brass frame, and the menu words are red small caps carved or painted on it.
- What works: diegetic. The menu is an object among other objects that tell you the world (tech plus magic) before you read a word.
- Idea for disc (title and new-game screens): a still life per faction behind the menu, such as an inquisitor's desk with seals and a censer, a noble's table with arcane batteries and a decanter, and a Sylvan altar of moss, bone and seed pods. The menu panel stands among them.

### Arcanum — new-character screen
- Image: `arcanum-char-creation-1.jpg`
- What's there: a portrait in a thin brass frame with round brass arrow buttons on both sides. Selector rows (gender, race, background) are black pills with red text and round brass arrows at both ends. A large black text panel with brass curly corner brackets holds race lore. Big round arrow buttons (red back, green forward) sit in the bottom corners.
- What works: one repeated selector form (pill plus two brass arrows) makes a long form calm.
- What doesn't: big flat black boxes, and the lore text is dense.
- Idea for disc (new-game screen): a single selector form used for every choice, with lore text in a separate plaque.

### Divine Divinity — angel and dragons that *are* the hotbar frame
- Image: `dd-hud-1.jpg` (bottom centre); `dd-hotbar-angel-detail.jpg`
- What's there: a compact bottom bar. In the middle of its top edge stands a small bronze angel whose **spread wings form the top rail** of the button panel on both sides; she occupies a slot between two button groups. At each end, a bronze **dragon wraps its body around the end of the bar**, coils looping, head turned inward and snout over the health (red) or mana (blue) bar. Buttons are carved ivory-stone squares with painted icons (map, book, hourglass, sword, ankh book, potion). A green and yellow stamina pair sits in the middle of the lower row.
- What works: this is the most direct answer to "the angels feel bolted on". The angel's wings *are* the frame's top edge, so remove her and the bar has no top. The dragons' coils are the bar's end caps. Each sculpture replaces a structural part instead of being added to one.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, ability bar and turn-order strip): put one angel at the centre of the ability bar whose **wings become the bar's top moulding**, with ability slots under the feathers. On Ral-Vitahl screens, battery cables or chains form the rail. On Sylvan screens, two roots or vines grow from the end caps and run along as the rail, ending in the bars' terminals.

### Divine Divinity — skill panel with a class statuette in a niche
- Image: `dd-skill-pentagrams-1.jpg` (top middle)
- What's there: a teal-and-pewter panel split into four skill groups ("Path of the thief / lore / talents / the divine"), with grey stone tablet slots. At the centre, between the groups, stands a **small statuette of the character in an arched niche**, a gothic shrine. Below it, a "Way of the survivor" selector bar has **small coiled serpents at its two ends** as end ornaments, and serpent knots fill the stat panel's lower corners. On the left, parchment sheets each show a pentagram with a race's emblem (teleporter list).
- What works: the statuette sits in a niche, a real architectural place for it, and it shows *your* class, so the ornament carries information. The serpents are used at joints and ends, where ornament belongs structurally.
- Idea for disc (unit card or codex): show the selected unit as a small statue in a niche at the centre of the card, with its stats in the four quadrants around it. Put ornament at joints (where two rails meet) and ends, never in the middle of a straight run.

### Divine Divinity — character selection as a lineup in the scene

### Blasphemous — medallion, a cherub on the frame, reliquary flasks
- Image: `blas1-hud-boss-1.jpg` (top left), `blas1-hud-cherub-detail.jpg`
- What's there: a round teal medallion with a sculpted face (guilt or portrait) sits in a gold baroque frame with flourishes. A **pink-flesh cherub clings to the frame** between the medallion and the second bar, as if pulling at it. The red health bar and blue fervour bar end in gold arrow tips. The bile flasks are **small glass lanterns or reliquaries hanging from gold caps**: red when full, clear and empty when used. The boss bar at the bottom is red segments in a gold frame with baroque end caps and a central crest, with the boss name above it in a pixel blackletter.
- What works: the cherub is *doing* something (clinging, climbing) with its body over the frame's join. It sits exactly where two frame pieces meet and hides the seam. The flasks are religious objects, so the resource belongs to the world's faith.
- Idea for disc (Jilliath, battle screen): put small angel or putti figures **at the joints** (where the turn-order strip meets the unit card, where the ability bar meets the log), gripping or bracing the seam. Charges or uses of abilities could be little hanging reliquaries or censers that empty.

### Blasphemous — rosary beads as equip slots
- Image: `blas1-rosary-1.jpg`
- What's there: the inventory grid is gold-cornered square cells. The equipped side is a **string of round gold-framed beads linked by small spheres**, an actual rosary, with the active one crowned by a gold ornament. ### Blasphemous 2 — altarpiece of paired statue slots
- Image: `blas2-altarpiece-1.jpg`
- What's there: four pairs of small bronze saint statues, each statue in its own gothic arched niche with a finial, and a golden flame burning between the two niches of each pair. The selected niche is gold-outlined. The right side lists bonuses with icons and a description plaque.
- What works: the slots are niches and the items are statues, so the screen is a carved altarpiece built from the items themselves.
- Idea for disc (capitol): a Jilliath capitol's patron saints or angels as statue slots in an altarpiece. A Sylvan grove's equivalent could be hollows in a trunk.

### Dungeon Keeper 1 — a single carved slab as the side panel
- Image: `dk1-panel-1.jpg` (left)
- What's there: the left panel is one rough sandstone slab with chipped edges. A **carved oval holds the minimap** with N/S/E/W letters in the stone. Below are the gold count in engraved numerals, a row of tab icons (question mark, red room, door, trap, imp), the selected spell with a cost bar, and a 4x4 grid of square stone buttons with room and creature icons. The cursor is a big green hand in the game view.
- What works: one material and one slab, so everything is carved into it and nothing is added. It feels like a tool you hold.
- Idea for disc (overworld side panels): make each side panel feel like **one carved slab** whose sections are recesses cut into it, not boxes framed and then decorated. For the Sylvan, the slab could be a single slice of trunk with growth rings, with the sections hollowed into the wood.

### Dungeon Keeper 2 — brass rails and a minimap in a stone square
- Image: `dk2-hud-1.jpg`
- What's there: a thin brass rod runs along the top and another along the bottom like curtain rails, with the panels hanging off them. Top left, a round dial with a heart (the dungeon heart) and green income numbers. Next to it, a round gold-coin dial with yellow numerals. Bottom left, a round minimap in a stone square with four corner buttons tucked into its corners. Big square stone buttons carry pale blue line icons (bed, chicken, books).
- What works: the four corner buttons fill the space a round map leaves in a square. The rails are a light structure that doesn't eat the game view.
- Idea for disc (overworld): fill the corners left by a round element (a medallion or a minimap) with four small round buttons. Hang the turn bar from a thin rod instead of putting it in a heavy box.

### Dungeon Keeper 2 — the menu and campaign map as a 3D room
- Image: `dk2-main-menu-1.jpg`, `dk2-campaign-map-1.jpg`
- What's there: the main menu floats in a 3D vaulted chamber with chained bronze lamps on brackets, a relief map table in the middle and a hand pointing. The campaign map is a **raised relief map on a stone slab**: coloured realms, a castle on a snowy mountain, small towers, a river, with the keeper's green hand pointing at the next realm and a red arrow.
- What works: the map is a physical model you lean over, which suits conquest.
- Idea for disc (new-game screen, campaign or scenario choice): show the world as a carved relief table with the factions' capitols as small models.

### Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain — portrait shrine side panel
- Image: `bo-hud-1.jpg` (right)
- What's there: a teal-green marble side panel about 1/4 of the screen wide. At its top, the hero's portrait stands in a **pointed gothic lancet niche** framed in red, flanked by **two teal stone skulls** at its shoulders. Below are an empty item well, a **red blood vial** in brass caps standing diagonally at the left, a sun or moon icon over a **square clock face** in the middle, and a blue magic orb at the right.
- What works: everything is set into one stone wall, with the portrait as the shrine's saint. The skulls frame the niche at its shoulders, where an architect would put corbels.
- Idea for disc (battle screen, unit card): the unit's portrait in a lancet niche. Put carved heads or skulls as corbels at the niche's shoulders, a faction-specific motif in a structural position.

### Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain — tabs as hooded statues holding their objects
- Image: `bo-inventory-1.jpg`, `bo-spell-menu-1.jpg`
- What's there: three **hooded statues in gothic niches** stand in a row. Each holds the object that names its tab: one an orb (spells), one a sword (weapons), one a book (artifacts). The selected statue turns violet in the spell menu. The niches are separated by **brass columns topped with gold skull capitals** and openwork brass tracery. Below, a large carved relief panel of two coiled dragons serves as the description field. A column of rune-carved stone tabs runs down the left. In the spell menu, a cross-shaped selector of spell pictures sits on the left, and on the right two bronze dragons stand as heraldic supporters either side of the chosen spell's picture.
- What works: **the statues are the tabs.** The figure holds the function, so it can't be decoration. The skulls are column capitals and the dragons are supporters, so every sculpture sits in an architectural role.
- Idea for disc (capitol tab rail): replace the round medallion tabs with **angel statues in niches, each holding the object of its tab** (a quill for the codex, a sword for recruiting, a key for the treasury, a scale for laws). For Ral-Vitahl use servants or automata holding objects. For the Sylvan, figures grown from wood, each holding a seed, a bone or a lantern. Flank the selected item's preview with two figures as supporters.

### Bloodborne — restraint
- Image: `bb-inventory-1.jpg`
- What's there: a dark, book-like slab with a slightly 3D edge. The ornament is only **thin silver rules ending in tiny curl terminals**. Tab tiles are small engraved-plate illustrations (hands, key, chalice, a hat) in silver-grey. The serif type is warm grey for headers and off-white for body, and stats are listed with small coloured icons.
- What works: it proves that a gothic feeling can come from type, rule terminals and engraved icons alone. Next to a heavy HUD, a calm screen like this rests the eye.
- Idea for disc (codex and battle log): keep text-heavy screens light, with thin rules and curl terminals, and spend sculpture on a few anchor objects only. That way the ornate pieces stay special.

## Patterns across these games

**1. Sculpture replaces a structural part instead of decorating one.** The strongest examples aren't "frame plus statue". Divine Divinity's angel wings *are* the bar's top rail and its dragons *are* the end caps. Blood Omen's skulls are column capitals and its statues are the tabs. VtM:R's mourners are the panel's side posts. PoE's statues hold the globes. The test: if you delete the figure, does the frame lose a part? If not, it's bolted on.

**2. Figures have a job and a pose that shows it.** They hold, carry, lean, cling or point. PoE's woman braces the globe, the Blasphemous cherub clings to a seam, Blood Omen's statues each hold their tab's object, the Dungeon Keeper hand points at the next realm. A figure standing in a corner, facing out, reads as a sticker.

**3. Overlap across the frame line.** Spears, arms, wings and tails cross *in front of* the frame edge (PoE), and portraits rise *behind* a window edge (PoE2 vendor). One overlap in each direction sells the depth. Disc's current angels are likely clipped inside the border-image, which is exactly what makes them feel stuck on.

**4. Put ornament at joints, ends and corbels, never mid-span.** Serpents sit at the ends of the DD selector bar, the cherub covers the Blasphemous seam, skulls sit at the Blood Omen niche shoulders, and a statuette marks the end of the PoE2 rail. Architects decorate where loads meet, so ornament placed there looks load-bearing.

**5. Unique pieces, not copies.** PoE's two globe statues are different people. VtM:R's pilaster has a different emblem on every block. Grim Dawn's constellations are all different drawings. Blood Omen's three statues share a style but each holds something different. Repetition happens at the *structural* level (the same niche, the same block, the same arch) while the *content* of each repeat changes. That's the direct answer to "less copies of the same thing".

**6. The relief as the wall.** PoE2's inventory is cut *into* a grisaille relief. Low-contrast, near-monochrome sculpture fills the background, and the content windows are cut out of it. It gives richness without competing with content, and it can never look attached.

**7. Organic ornament as material, not attachment.** VtM:R's blood pools and drips over the frame's bottom edge as a stain. Divine Divinity's dragon coils wrap around the bar's ends. For the Sylvan this suggests rot, moss and sap *staining and growing over* the frame, and roots that *are* the rails and end caps, not carved roots glued to corners.

**8. Each resource gets its own instrument.** Arcanum uses a tube, a dial, a thermometer and a sky window. VtM:R uses blood vials and enamel medallions. Blasphemous uses hanging reliquaries. Grim Dawn uses clamped glass tubes. You learn values by shape, and the instrument tells the lore (batteries for Ral-Vitahl, reliquaries for Jilliath, seed pods or sap for the Sylvan).

**9. Rarity and type live in a header plate, not the whole frame.** PoE's tooltip headers change colour and end ornaments by rarity while the body stays the same. Grim Dawn colours the item name. Cell background tint shows state (PoE2 vendor red cells, Grim Dawn tinted cells, PoE stash). That gives one family with many variants.

**10. Screens stay one family through material and type, and differ through one hero object.** PoE runs on bronze plus dark stone plus gold plaques everywhere, then gives each screen a hero object: a carved board (temple), a relief (inventory), a painted roundel (tree), a cabinet (stash). Grim Dawn gives each screen its own picture (constellations, a map scroll, a class card). Disc's screens could share the iron, stone and marble kit and each get one unique centrepiece.

**11. Diegetic menus.** Arcanum's still life, Dungeon Keeper 2's chamber and relief map table, the Divine Divinity class lineup in the forest, the VtM:R stained-glass window. The title and new-game screens are the cheapest place for this and set the tone.

**12. Text treatment.** Small caps with thin fading rules (PoE), blackletter only for place names (Grim Dawn), lowercase uncial for menus (VtM:R), dotted underlines for defined terms (PoE2), engraved-plate icons (Bloodborne). Restraint on text-heavy screens makes the sculpted ones land.

**13. HUD to game-view boundary.** It's handled two ways. Heavy pieces sit only at the corners or ends and stay thin in between (PoE, Grim Dawn, Divine Divinity: the bar is thin, the globes and dragons are big). Or one side of the screen is a full slab (Dungeon Keeper 1, Blood Omen, VtM:R), and then the slab has a single material. Mixing the two, a heavy frame all round, is what none of them do.

(From memory, not in the screenshots: Thief: The Dark Project's "light gem", a single jewel at the bottom centre of the screen whose brightness shows how visible you are, is the purest example of one bespoke object carrying a hidden state. Black & White has almost no HUD: the god's hand is the cursor and information lives on scrolls and signposts in the world. Both are worth a look for disc's turn bar and unit status.)
